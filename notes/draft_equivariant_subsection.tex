\subsection{Equivariant message passing: when rotations matter}
\label{subsec:equivariant-mp}

Astrophysical signals are drenched in rotational structure: velocities, shears, spins, alignments. Permutation symmetry, which every GNN respects by construction, says nothing about these, and a network fed raw vectors happily learns functions that depend on the orientation of the simulation box. Three roads lead to a model that respects the rotation group, and the choice among them is a modelling decision, not a technicality.

To build the symmetry into the architecture is the first road. The equivariant GNN of \citet{Satorras2021_EGNN_equivariant} keeps two ledgers per node, invariant scalars and equivariant vectors: messages are scalar functions of invariant quantities such as pairwise distances, and positions or velocities update only along difference vectors, each scaled by such a scalar. Every internal quantity then transforms correctly under E(3) by construction, and the guarantee holds at test time no matter what the network learned. The intrinsic-alignment model of \citet{Jagvaral2022_intrinsicalignment_GNN} runs on exactly this principle.

To feed the network nothing but invariants is the second road. Replace raw positions by relative separations and raw velocities by their moduli, and any architecture becomes rotation-invariant through its diet rather than its anatomy; this is the route of the field-level inference of \citet{deSanti2023_fieldlevel_LFI}, and much of its cross-simulation robustness travels through that choice. The road costs nothing to implement and discards the directional information the task may or may not need.

To augment the training data with random rotations is the third road: cheapest to set up, and the only one of the three that offers no guarantee, since the network merely sees many orientations and is asked to infer the lesson. When the symmetry is exact and matters, architecture beats diet beats augmentation in the strength of the promise, and the order reverses in the cost of implementation. When the symmetry is only approximate, a survey with a preferred line of sight, redshift-space distortions, a selection function, the soft-constraint route of \S~\ref{subsec:bp-bias} reenters, and the strict architecture can even mislead. The equivariant family runs deeper than we need here, through tensor features and spherical harmonics, and \citet{Satorras2021_EGNN_equivariant} is the practical entry point for the astrophysical reader.
